Facade concept

The road front is split into three parts that follow the plan. The left wing sits in a white frame with a two-storey open loggia at its west end, where the first-floor outdoor dining sits under the gym. The core is a sandstone portal holding the double-height reception glazing, the recessed meditation lawn and the entrance canopy, with the name on its head beam. The right wing wraps the double-height badminton and squash hall in a perforated bronze screen that glows at night, above a banquet hall behind vertical fins.

Facade concept

Concept board 01 translated onto the actual plan massing and levels. The left wing's upper floors sit in a charcoal-framed box wrapped in warm-lit timber battens, cantilevered 2.2 m over a fully glazed ground floor with a timber soffit and linear lights. A deep timber canopy runs across the double-height atrium to a full-height textured white stone wall. The right wing is a limestone frame around a large glazed box with timber fins and greenery hanging from the roof edge. Planted ledges, a terrace pergola and a circular drop-off complete it. The board shows roughly G+2; the model follows the plans at G+3, so both boxes read taller.

05 · Decision path

From concept to coordinated elevation

What happens once the founders pick a direction.

01 · PLANRespect the floor plates

Keep the retail and clinic wing, central reception and circulation, banquet wing and the documented upper-floor uses. Check the road frontage and entry against the ground-floor drawing.

02 · SECTIONResolve real heights

Verify floor-to-floor levels, sports hall clear heights, parapets, terrace access, structural edges and service screens against Section S01 and consultant drawings.

03 · FACADECoordinate the materials

Choose one design language, then prepare measured elevations, glazing schedules, a shading and daylight study, material mock-ups, a maintenance strategy and a costed facade package.
